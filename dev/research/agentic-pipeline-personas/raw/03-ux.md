# Raw research — UX, design & accessibility

Axis: the UX/product-design quality inputs that must be present in the PRD and that constrain the Architecture and Tasks stages. Scope: interaction design (incl. empty/error/loading states), information architecture, journeys/story maps from the UX side, service blueprints, content design, WCAG 2.2 AA and inclusive design, design systems, evaluative usability research. Discovery-side generative research (interviews, JTBD) is covered in `01-discovery.md` and is referenced, not repeated.

Tagging convention: [WEB:<url>] = confirmed online during this research (mostly via search-result summaries, because w3.org, nngroup.com, gov.uk, dequeuniversity.com, inclusive.microsoft.design, smaply.com and atomicdesign.bradfrost.com were blocked by the egress proxy; the tag points at the URL that surfaced the claim); [BOOK:...] = from a book/standard known well; [UNVERIFIED] = plausible but not confirmed. No exact quotes unless the wording appeared in a search result.

## Literature & frameworks

### 1. Jakob Nielsen — 10 Usability Heuristics (1994; refreshed wording 2020) and heuristic evaluation
- The ten: (1) visibility of system status, (2) match between system and the real world, (3) user control and freedom, (4) consistency and standards, (5) error prevention, (6) recognition rather than recall, (7) flexibility and efficiency of use, (8) aesthetic and minimalist design, (9) help users recognize, diagnose and recover from errors, (10) help and documentation. [WEB:https://principles.design/examples/10-usability-heuristics-for-user-interface-design] [WEB:https://blog.uxtweak.com/usability-heuristics/]
- H1 maps directly to **loading/progress/status states**: the system should keep users informed through appropriate feedback within reasonable time. [WEB:https://blog.uxtweak.com/usability-heuristics/]
- H9 sets a testable bar for **error messages**: plain language (no codes), precisely state the problem, constructively suggest a solution. [WEB:https://blog.uxtweak.com/usability-heuristics/] H5 says prevention (constraints, good defaults, confirmations) beats good error messages.
- H3 (user control) maps to undo, cancel, back, "emergency exit" — every destructive or long-running action needs one. [BOOK:Nielsen, Usability Engineering, 1993, ch. 5] 
- **Severity ratings 0-4** (0 not a problem, 1 cosmetic, 2 minor, 3 major/high priority, 4 catastrophe — imperative to fix before release), judged on frequency, impact, persistence (+ market impact). [WEB:https://www.koji.so/docs/usability-issue-severity-ratings] [WEB:https://measuringu.com/rating-severity/]
- Single-evaluator severity ratings are unreliable; Nielsen recommends aggregating several evaluators (classic guidance: 3-5 evaluators find most heuristic problems). [WEB:https://www.koji.so/docs/usability-issue-severity-ratings] [BOOK:Nielsen & Mack, Usability Inspection Methods, 1994, ch. 2]
- Persona implication: heuristic review is a cheap **pre-PRD-exit inspection** an LLM can run on flows/wireframe descriptions, but must emit findings as `{heuristic, location, severity 0-4, evidence, fix}`; severity ≥3 blocks exit. Running multiple independent critic passes mimics multiple evaluators.

### 2. Jeff Patton — *User Story Mapping* (2014)
- Backbone = user activities left-to-right in narrative order; below it, user tasks/steps; details and variants hang vertically; horizontal slices = releases (walking skeleton / MVP first). [BOOK:Patton, User Story Mapping, 2014, ch. 1-3]
- "The map is a conversation tool" — shared understanding, not documentation; stories are placeholders for conversations. [BOOK:Patton, User Story Mapping, 2014, ch. "The Big Picture"]
- Every slice must be an end-to-end usable path ("minimum viable" means a user can complete the journey), not one fully-built column. [BOOK:Patton, 2014, ch. 2 "Plan to Build Less"]
- UX-side enrichment: each backbone step gets its alternate/unhappy paths (errors, empty, permission denied, offline) as explicit cards — otherwise they never get estimated. [UNVERIFIED — practitioner convention]
- Persona implication: the PRD stage's story map is the spine for traceability (journey step → requirement → screen/state → task). Tasks stage can refuse a slice that is not end-to-end.

### 3. Customer journey maps and service blueprints (Shostack 1984; Bitner, Ostrom & Morgan 2008; Gibbons/NN/g 2017)
- Blueprint lanes: customer actions, frontstage actions (visible), backstage actions (invisible), support processes; separated by line of interaction, **line of visibility**, line of internal interaction; plus a physical-evidence/touchpoint row. [WEB:https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/] (search summary) [BOOK:Bitner, Ostrom & Morgan, "Service Blueprinting: A Practical Technique for Service Innovation", California Management Review, 2008]
- Origin: G. Lynn Shostack, "Designing Services That Deliver", HBR 1984 (fail points, wait points). [BOOK:Shostack, HBR, 1984]
- The "aha" of blueprinting is discovering a backstage dependency owned by another team. [WEB:https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/] (search summary, Gibbons)
- Why it matters for this pipeline: a blueprint is the **natural bridge from PRD to Architecture** — the backstage/support lanes are the systems, queues, people and SLAs the architect must design; fail points become required error states and SRE alerts.
- Journey map (current/future state) = one persona, one scenario, phases, actions, thoughts, emotions, pain points, opportunities; it is NOT a blueprint (no backstage). [BOOK:Kalbach, Mapping Experiences, 2nd ed. 2020, ch. 1-2]
- *This is Service Design Doing* (Stickdorn et al., 2018) — blueprints and journeys as living, research-based artifacts; assumption-based maps must be labelled as such. [BOOK:Stickdorn, Hormess, Lawrence & Schneider, This Is Service Design Doing, 2018, ch. 4]

### 4. Information architecture — Rosenfeld, Morville & Arango (*IA for the Web and Beyond*, 4th ed. 2015); Abby Covert (*How to Make Sense of Any Mess*, 2014)
- Four systems: organization, labeling, navigation, search. [BOOK:Rosenfeld, Morville & Arango, 2015, Part II]
- IA is driven by the triad **users × content × context**. [BOOK:Rosenfeld et al., 2015, ch. 2]
- Artifacts: sitemap/app map, content inventory & audit, controlled vocabulary/taxonomy, navigation model, labeling system; validated by **card sorting** (generative) and **tree testing** (evaluative; task success on a text-only hierarchy). [BOOK:Rosenfeld et al., 2015; Spencer, Card Sorting, 2009]
- Covert: agree on language first — an ontology/glossary of terms, because "mess" is mostly unshared definitions. [BOOK:Covert, How to Make Sense of Any Mess, 2014]
- Persona implication: the **glossary/controlled vocabulary is a shared artifact** used by content design (UI labels), product (requirements) and architecture (domain model / DDD ubiquitous language). Mismatches between UI labels and domain entity names are a cross-stage defect.

### 5. Interaction design states — "the UI stack" and state completeness
- Scott Hurff's **UI stack**: every screen has five states — ideal, empty, error, partial, loading. [BOOK:Hurff, Designing Products People Love, 2016, ch. on UI stack] [UNVERIFIED as to chapter]
- Empty states have three kinds: first-use (onboarding/call to action), user-cleared (inbox zero), no-results (search/filter) — each needs different content. [UNVERIFIED — common practitioner taxonomy, e.g. Material Design guidance]
- Cooper's *About Face* (4th ed. 2014): goal-directed design; personas → scenarios → requirements → framework; "excise" (work that does not serve the goal) should be eliminated; don't use modal error dialogs to punish users. [BOOK:Cooper, Reimann, Cronin & Noessel, About Face, 2014, Part I and ch. on errors]
- Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (rev. 2013): affordances/signifiers, feedback, mapping, constraints, conceptual model; slips vs mistakes — design for error (make actions reversible, make errors detectable). [BOOK:Norman, DOET, 2013, ch. 1, 5]
- Testable bar used later in this file: **state matrix** = for each screen/component × {ideal, loading, empty (by kind), partial, error (by class: validation, permission, not-found, conflict, network/offline, server, timeout), success/confirmation}. Missing cells are defects.

### 6. Content design — Sarah Richards (*Content Design*, 2017) and GOV.UK
- Content design was developed by Richards' team at GDS (2010-2014) while merging hundreds of government sites into GOV.UK; she was head of content design 2011-2014. [WEB:https://apolitical.co/en/articles/how-the-uk-transformed-digital-content]
- Content design is not "writing copy for pages"; it is starting from a **user need** and choosing the best format (which may be a tool, a table, or nothing at all) to meet it as fast as possible. [WEB:https://www.cypres.com/en/insight/govuk-content-lead-sarah-richards-turning-complexity-simplicity] [BOOK:Richards, Content Design, 2017, ch. 1]
- Publish only what users need: no jargon, no legal caveats in the way of the task; use the users' language (search data, interviews). [WEB:https://thedutchess.be/2017-11-16-creating-govuk-with-content-design/]
- Artifacts: user-need statements ("As a… I need… so that…"), **job stories**, content inventory/audit, pair-writing sessions, a style guide, and "2i" (second-pair-of-eyes) review. [BOOK:Richards, Content Design, 2017, ch. 3-7] [WEB:https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2012/07/02/gov-uk-editorial-style-guide]
- Readability: front-load, short sentences, plain English, target ~reading age 9 for GOV.UK mainstream content (practitioner guideline). [UNVERIFIED for exact figure]
- Complement: Torrey Podmajersky, *Strategic Writing for UX* (2019) — voice chart; UI text patterns (titles, buttons, errors, empty states, notifications); errors must say what happened + how to fix, in the voice. [BOOK:Podmajersky, Strategic Writing for UX, 2019, ch. 4-5]; Kinneret Yifrah, *Microcopy* (2017).
- Persona implication: content designer owns the **string/message inventory** — every error/empty/confirmation state from the state matrix has content, not "TBD" and not "Something went wrong".

### 7. WCAG 2.2 (W3C Recommendation, Oct 2023) and accessibility practice
- Four principles **POUR**: Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust; conformance levels A, AA, AAA; AA is the common legal/contract target (EN 301 549 v3.2.1 references WCAG 2.1 AA; Section 508 references 2.0 AA; EU European Accessibility Act applies from 28 June 2025). [WEB:https://www.audioeye.com/post/wcag-22/] [BOOK:W3C, WCAG 2.2, 2023] [UNVERIFIED for legal-mapping details per jurisdiction]
- WCAG 2.2 adds 9 SCs; **4.1.1 Parsing is obsolete/removed**. [WEB:https://www.sitebrunch.com/en/facts/wcag-2-2] New ones: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) AA; 2.4.12 Focus Not Obscured (Enhanced) AAA; 2.4.13 Focus Appearance AAA; 2.5.7 Dragging Movements AA; 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) AA — 24×24 CSS px or sufficient spacing; 3.2.6 Consistent Help A; 3.3.7 Redundant Entry A; 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum) AA — no cognitive function test (memorizing password, puzzle) unless alternative/assist; 3.3.9 Accessible Authentication (Enhanced) AAA. [WEB:https://www.audioeye.com/post/wcag-22/] [WEB:https://www.sitebrunch.com/en/facts/wcag-2-2]
- High-frequency AA criteria that must be designed upstream (not "fixed in QA"): 1.1.1 non-text alternatives; 1.3.1 info & relationships; 1.4.3 contrast 4.5:1 text / 3:1 large; 1.4.11 non-text contrast 3:1; 1.4.10 reflow at 320 CSS px; 1.4.4 resize text 200%; 2.1.1 keyboard; 2.4.3 focus order; 2.4.7 focus visible; 3.3.1 error identification; 3.3.2 labels or instructions; 3.3.3 error suggestion; 4.1.2 name, role, value; 4.1.3 status messages (so loading/success/error announcements reach screen readers). [BOOK:W3C, WCAG 2.1/2.2 SC text]
- Many SCs are **requirements on states**: 3.3.1/3.3.3 (error state), 4.1.3 (loading/success status), 2.2.1 timing adjustable (timeouts), 3.3.7 (multi-step flows), 3.3.8 (login architecture). Accessibility is therefore a PRD + Architecture concern (auth method, component library, SPA focus management), not just a front-end checklist.
- Automated tools (axe-core, Lighthouse) catch only a minority of WCAG failures; manual keyboard and screen-reader testing is required. [UNVERIFIED for the commonly cited ~30-40% figure]
- WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices Guide (APG): use native HTML first; "no ARIA is better than bad ARIA". [BOOK:W3C WAI, ARIA Authoring Practices Guide]

### 8. Inclusive design — Microsoft Inclusive Design toolkit (2016); Kat Holmes, *Mismatch* (2018)
- Three principles: **recognize exclusion; learn from diversity; solve for one, extend to many**. [WEB:https://inclusive.microsoft.design/articles/inclusive-101-guidebook] (search summary)
- Disability is a **mismatched human interaction**, not a personal health condition. [WEB:https://inclusive.microsoft.design/articles/inclusive-101-guidebook] (search summary) [BOOK:Holmes, Mismatch, 2018, ch. 1]
- **Persona spectrum**: permanent / temporary / situational (one arm / arm injury / holding a baby). [WEB:https://inclusive.microsoft.design/articles/inclusive-101-guidebook] (search summary)
- Microsoft extended the toolkit to cognition (Inclusive Design for Cognition guidebook, 2023). [WEB:https://inclusive.microsoft.design/tools-and-activities/InclusiveDesignForCognitionGuidebook.pdf] [WEB:https://www.fastcompany.com/90859704/microsofts-new-inclusive-design-toolkit-designed-for-the-brain]
- Persona implication: inclusive design widens the PRD's personas/constraints (low bandwidth, one-handed use, noisy env, low literacy, non-native language) beyond WCAG conformance; WCAG = floor, inclusive design = method.

### 9. Design systems — Brad Frost, *Atomic Design* (2016); Alla Kholmatova, *Design Systems* (2017); DTCG tokens
- Atomic design: atoms → molecules → organisms → templates → pages. [BOOK:Frost, Atomic Design, 2016, ch. 2]
- Kholmatova: functional patterns vs perceptual patterns; shared language is the core of a system; strict vs loose, modular vs integrated, centralized vs distributed. [BOOK:Kholmatova, Design Systems, Smashing, 2017]
- **Design Tokens Format Module 2025.10** — first stable DTCG spec (28 Oct 2025): tokens as JSON with `$value` and `$type`, aliases by reference, theming/multi-brand, `.tokens.json`. [WEB:https://www.w3.org/community/design-tokens/2025/10/28/design-tokens-specification-reaches-first-stable-version/] [WEB:https://design-tokens.github.io/community-group/format/]
- A component in a mature system ships with: anatomy, variants, **all states** (default, hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, error, empty), usage do/don't, content guidelines, accessibility notes (keyboard, ARIA, contrast), and code. [UNVERIFIED — consolidated from public systems e.g. GOV.UK Design System, Carbon, Material]
- Persona implication: Architecture must declare the component library/token source; Tasks must reference components by name; "net-new component" is an explicit, costed decision with a11y review, never implicit.

### 10. Evaluative research — usability testing
- Steve Krug, *Rocket Surgery Made Easy* (2010) / *Don't Make Me Think* (2014): small, frequent tests (3 users, monthly); focus on fixing the worst problems first. [BOOK:Krug, Rocket Surgery Made Easy, 2010, ch. 2-3, 11]
- Nielsen's "5 users" (1993/2000): ~85% of problems found by 5 users per homogeneous segment *for qualitative testing*; iterate rather than one big test; quantitative benchmarks need ~20 per segment. [BOOK:Nielsen & Landauer, 1993] [WEB:https://www.nngroup.com/articles/quantitative-studies-how-many-users/] (the ~20 recommendation is Nielsen's own, NN/g "Quantitative Studies: How Many Users to Test?")
- Metrics: task success, time on task, errors, SUS (System Usability Scale, Brooke — created 1986, published 1996; 10 items, average ≈68), SEQ, UMUX-Lite. [BOOK:Sauro & Lewis, Quantifying the User Experience, 2nd ed. 2016]
- Standard artifacts: research plan (questions, method, participants/recruiting screener, tasks with success criteria, metrics), discussion guide, findings report with severity and evidence (clips/quotes), rainbow spreadsheet. [BOOK:Rubin & Chisnell, Handbook of Usability Testing, 2nd ed. 2008, ch. 5]
- Persona implication: an LLM cannot run a real usability test. It *can* author the plan, tasks, success criteria and a heuristic/cognitive walkthrough, and it *must* label any "findings" not from real participants as `simulated/inspection`, never as validated evidence.

### 11. DesignOps — Kate Kaplan / NN/g; Dave Malouf
- DesignOps = orchestration and optimization of people, processes and craft to amplify design's value and impact at scale. [WEB:https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-ops-definitions/] (search summary)
- Typical layers: how we work together (process, rituals, critiques, handoff), how we get work done (tools, templates, file/asset governance), how our work creates impact (measurement). [WEB:https://www.nngroup.com/articles/start-designops/] (search summary) [UNVERIFIED for exact layer names]
- Persona implication: in the agentic pipeline DesignOps ≈ **the owner of templates, Definition of Ready/Done for design, the critique protocol and the handoff spec format** — closer to a harness/process role than a craft role.

## Real-world roles

### R1. Product Designer (UX / Interaction Designer, incl. IA)
- **Titles**: Product Designer, UX Designer, Interaction Designer, UX Architect / Information Architect (IA often folded in at product companies). **Stages**: discovery (prototypes for risk tests), **PRD (primary)**, architecture (consulted), tasks (consulted on UI tasks).
- **Mission**: Turn validated problems into flows, IA and interaction specs that are complete across all states and usable by the target users, so PRD requirements are buildable and testable.
- **Mindset & principles**: goal-directed over feature-directed [BOOK:Cooper et al., About Face, 2014]; design for error — prevention first, recovery second [BOOK:Norman, DOET, 2013, ch. 5]; system status always visible [WEB:https://blog.uxtweak.com/usability-heuristics/]; consistency via design system, not novelty [BOOK:Kholmatova, 2017]; slices must be end-to-end usable [BOOK:Patton, 2014]; owns usability risk in the product trio [BOOK:Cagan, Inspired, 2017].
- **OWNS**: user flows (happy + unhappy paths), IA (app map, navigation, labels in collaboration with content), wireframes/prototypes, the **state matrix**, interaction specs (behaviour, validation timing, focus management intent), UX acceptance criteria per story.
- **DOES NOT OWN**: problem validation and prioritization (PM/discovery); final strings (content designer); WCAG conformance sign-off (a11y specialist); component code and API design (engineering); research evidence quality (UX researcher).
- **Inputs needed**: discovery handoff (problem statement, personas/JTBD, outcomes, risk register with usability risks flagged); constraints (platforms, devices, locales, a11y target); design system inventory; known backend capabilities/limits.
- **Outputs (industry formats)**: user-flow diagrams (boxes = screens/states, diamonds = decisions, explicit error edges); app/sitemap; annotated wireframes or Figma prototype; **state matrix** table (screen × state); interaction spec / "redlines" or Dev Mode annotations; per-story UX acceptance criteria in Given/When/Then.
- **ACCEPTANCE criteria**:
  - [ ] Every flow in scope traces to a journey/story-map step and a PRD requirement ID.
  - [ ] Every screen has ideal, loading, empty (typed: first-use / no-results / cleared), partial, and error states defined; every error class reachable from the backend is mapped to a UI state with recovery action.
  - [ ] Every destructive action has confirm or undo; every long-running action (>1 s nominal) has progress indication and cancel where feasible. [BOOK:Nielsen, H1/H3]
  - [ ] Form validation timing (inline vs on submit), required-field marking, and preserved input after error are specified.
  - [ ] Navigation model and labels are listed; every label is in the shared glossary.
  - [ ] Components used exist in the design system or are flagged `net-new` with justification.
  - [ ] Heuristic self-review done; no open severity-3/4 findings.
  - [ ] Responsive/reflow behaviour at 320 CSS px and 200% zoom described for each layout. [BOOK:WCAG 1.4.10/1.4.4]
- **REFUSAL criteria**:
  - *blocked*: no validated problem statement or target persona; no stated platform/device scope; no a11y conformance target; backend error classes unknown and no one to ask (cannot design error states).
  - *rejected*: upstream PRD/discovery prescribes UI solutions as requirements without the underlying need ("add a dropdown"); story map slices that are not end-to-end; requirements with no success metric making usability acceptance untestable.
  - *rejected (own output, will not ship)*: any flow lacking error and empty states; any flow with a dead end (no exit/back); "Something went wrong" as the only error design.
  - *out_of_scope*: brand/marketing visuals, GTM messaging, pricing pages copy strategy; backend data model decisions; legal text drafting.
- **Anti-patterns**: happy-path-only Figma; "pixel-perfect" mockups with no behaviour spec; inventing components per screen; designing against an imagined backend that can't return the needed data; modal-error everything; disabled submit buttons with no explanation.
- **Handoffs**: receives from Discovery orchestrator (problem, personas, risks), UX researcher (evidence), service designer (blueprint); delivers to PRD orchestrator (flows/state matrix), content designer (state list needing strings), a11y specialist (review), Architecture (states ⇒ API error contract & latency budget), Tasks (per-screen UI tasks).

### R2. Service Designer
- **Titles**: Service Designer, CX/Journey Designer, Service Architect. **Stages**: discovery (current-state blueprint), **PRD (future-state blueprint)**, **architecture (backstage/support lanes)**.
- **Mission**: Make the whole service — front- and back-stage, people, systems, policies — coherent, so the product UI is not a facade over a broken process.
- **Mindset & principles**: services are co-produced and span channels; design the backstage, not just the screen [BOOK:Stickdorn et al., 2018]; surface fail points and wait points [BOOK:Shostack, HBR, 1984]; the value is revealing cross-team dependencies [WEB:https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/].
- **OWNS**: journey maps (current/future), service blueprint, touchpoint inventory, fail-point register, ownership of each backstage step.
- **DOES NOT OWN**: system architecture choices; screen-level interaction design; org change execution.
- **Inputs**: discovery evidence; org/process info (who does what today); system landscape; SLAs/policies.
- **Outputs**: blueprint (lanes: evidence/touchpoints, customer actions, frontstage, backstage, support processes; lines of interaction/visibility/internal interaction; annotated fail points, wait times, metrics); journey map (phases, actions, thoughts/feelings, pain points, opportunities).
- **ACCEPTANCE**:
  - [ ] Each frontstage step has at least one backstage/support step or is explicitly self-contained.
  - [ ] Each backstage step names an owner (team/system) and expected latency/SLA.
  - [ ] Fail points are marked and each one maps to a UI error/recovery state and an operational alert or manual process.
  - [ ] Offline/human channels (support, email, phone) are represented where users actually use them.
  - [ ] Map is labelled `evidence-based` or `assumption-based` per lane.
- **REFUSAL**:
  - *blocked*: no information about backstage processes/owners for a service with human or third-party steps.
  - *rejected*: architecture that drops a backstage step present in the blueprint without a decision record; journey maps with no evidence presented as validated.
  - *out_of_scope*: organizational restructuring, staffing plans, marketing journey/funnel (GTM).
- **Anti-patterns**: blueprint as wall art never updated; "journey map" that is really a marketing funnel; ignoring internal staff experience; blueprint stopping at the line of visibility.
- **Handoffs**: from Discovery (evidence) → to Product Designer (frontstage), Architecture orchestrator (backstage/support = components, integrations, queues, SLAs), SRE/operability pool (fail points ⇒ alerts/runbooks), traceability owner.

### R3. UX Researcher (evaluative)
- **Titles**: UX Researcher, Usability Specialist, Research Ops (supporting). **Stages**: discovery (generative — see 01), **PRD exit (evaluative)**, tasks/post-build (validation plan), shared (evidence quality).
- **Mission**: Produce trustworthy evidence on whether target users can complete the designed tasks, and keep the line between evidence and opinion sharp.
- **Mindset & principles**: test early and often with few users [BOOK:Krug, 2010]; iterate tests rather than one large test [BOOK:Nielsen & Landauer, 1993]; define task success criteria before testing [BOOK:Rubin & Chisnell, 2008]; severity = frequency × impact × persistence [WEB:https://measuringu.com/rating-severity/]; one evaluator's severity is unreliable [WEB:https://www.koji.so/docs/usability-issue-severity-ratings].
- **OWNS**: research plan, screener, tasks & scenarios, success metrics, heuristic evaluation/cognitive walkthrough (inspection), findings with severity, research repository entries.
- **DOES NOT OWN**: the design fix; prioritization of fixes (PM with designer); recruiting logistics at scale (ResearchOps).
- **Inputs**: research questions tied to risks; prototype or flow spec; personas/segments; success metrics.
- **Outputs**: research plan (objectives, questions, method, participants n per segment, tasks w/ success criteria, metrics: task success, time, errors, SUS/SEQ); discussion guide; findings report (issue, evidence, severity 0-4, affected heuristic/SC, recommendation); usability benchmark targets for the PRD (e.g., "≥80% unassisted task success on task T1").
- **ACCEPTANCE**:
  - [ ] Every finding cites evidence source (participant id/clip, or `inspection` method + evaluator count).
  - [ ] Severity uses a declared scale; ≥2 independent raters for severity ≥3 (for agents: ≥2 independent critic passes).
  - [ ] Tasks are realistic scenarios, not instructions that leak the UI label ("click Export").
  - [ ] Participants include at least one assistive-technology user or a stated plan to (inclusive sampling).
  - [ ] PRD contains measurable usability acceptance targets for the critical tasks.
- **REFUSAL**:
  - *blocked*: no research question or no artifact to evaluate; no definition of target users.
  - *rejected*: "validated by users" claims with no study; leading tasks; SUS with n<5 reported as a benchmark; synthetic/LLM-simulated users presented as real evidence.
  - *out_of_scope*: market sizing, pricing research, A/B experiment platform design (product analytics pool).
- **Anti-patterns**: confirmation research ("prove the design works"); opinion surveys instead of task observation; findings without severity; research reports nobody acts on.
- **Handoffs**: from Product Designer (prototype/flows), Discovery (risks) → to PRD orchestrator (findings, usability targets), Product Designer (fixes), product analytics pool (post-launch metrics).

### R4. Content Designer (UX Writer)
- **Titles**: Content Designer, UX Writer, Content Strategist, Content Designer (Accessibility). **Stages**: discovery (user language), **PRD (primary)**, tasks (string files/i18n keys).
- **Mission**: Meet user needs with the right content in the right format, in the users' language, including every message the system says in error, empty and success states.
- **Mindset & principles**: start from the user need, not the page [BOOK:Richards, Content Design, 2017]; publish only what users need, no jargon [WEB:https://thedutchess.be/2017-11-16-creating-govuk-with-content-design/]; errors say what happened and how to fix, no blame, no codes [WEB:https://blog.uxtweak.com/usability-heuristics/]; consistent voice via voice chart [BOOK:Podmajersky, 2019]; content is design, not decoration.
- **OWNS**: content inventory & string catalogue (key, context, text, character limits, variables, pluralization notes), voice & tone guidance, glossary/terminology (shared with IA), error/empty/confirmation message library, alt-text guidance, readability targets.
- **DOES NOT OWN**: legal/regulatory text (legal, privacy pool — content designer may plain-language it with approval); translation execution; marketing copy (GTM, out of scope).
- **Inputs**: state matrix; user language evidence (interview transcripts, search terms, support tickets); glossary; locale list; character constraints from components.
- **Outputs**: string table (`key | screen/state | text | max chars | placeholders | notes for translators`); message patterns (error: what happened + why if useful + what to do; empty: what this is + how to start); glossary; style guide deltas; content crit/2i record.
- **ACCEPTANCE**:
  - [ ] Every cell of the state matrix that renders text has a final string (no "Lorem", no "TBD", no "Error 500").
  - [ ] Every error message names the problem in user terms and the recovery action; validation errors reference the field label (supports WCAG 3.3.1/3.3.3).
  - [ ] Every empty state explains what will appear and gives a primary action (first-use) or way out (no-results: clear filters/search tips).
  - [ ] Terminology matches the glossary; one term per concept across UI, PRD and API-facing messages.
  - [ ] Button labels are verbs describing the outcome; link text is meaningful out of context (WCAG 2.4.4).
  - [ ] Strings are externalized with i18n keys, placeholders and plural forms; text-expansion (~30-40% for some locales) considered [UNVERIFIED for exact %].
  - [ ] Readability target declared and met (e.g., plain-English check).
- **REFUSAL**:
  - *blocked*: no state list / flows to write for; no user-language evidence AND no glossary (would be guessing terms).
  - *rejected*: generic error copy ("Oops! Something went wrong") for recoverable errors; strings hard-coded in designs with no keys; legal text inserted that blocks the task with no plain-language summary; jargon/internal system names in UI.
  - *out_of_scope*: campaign/marketing copy, release notes for GTM, translation itself.
- **Anti-patterns**: copy written last to fit boxes; humour in error states; inconsistent synonyms ("workspace/project/space"); "click here"; content for stakeholders not users.
- **Handoffs**: from Product Designer (state matrix), UX researcher (user language) → to PRD orchestrator (string catalogue), a11y specialist (labels, alt text), Tasks (i18n resource tasks), Architecture (error contract must carry codes that map to user messages — API should not ship user-facing text blobs).

### R5. Accessibility Specialist
- **Titles**: Accessibility Specialist/Lead, Digital Accessibility Engineer, A11y Program Manager, IAAP CPACC/WAS-certified auditor. **Stages**: **shared pool** (invoked at PRD exit, Architecture exit, Tasks DoD). (Also appears in the cross-cutting pool definition; this is the UX-side profile.)
- **Mission**: Ensure the product conforms to WCAG 2.2 AA (or stated target) by design, and that people with disabilities can actually complete the core tasks.
- **Mindset & principles**: shift-left — accessibility is a requirement, not a QA phase; POUR [WEB:https://www.audioeye.com/post/wcag-22/]; native semantics before ARIA [BOOK:W3C WAI APG]; disability as mismatch, persona spectrum [WEB:https://inclusive.microsoft.design/articles/inclusive-101-guidebook]; automated checks are necessary but not sufficient [UNVERIFIED for coverage %]; conformance is per page/flow — one failing step fails the flow (WCAG "complete processes" conformance requirement) [BOOK:W3C, WCAG 2.2, Conformance §5.2.3].
- **OWNS**: a11y requirements in PRD (conformance target, AT/browser matrix), annotation review (headings, landmarks, focus order, accessible names, keyboard interactions, announcements), WCAG audit and VPAT/ACR (Accessibility Conformance Report) when needed, a11y acceptance criteria per story, a11y test plan.
- **DOES NOT OWN**: visual design decisions (advises); legal interpretation of obligations (legal/compliance pool); fixing code (engineering).
- **Inputs**: conformance target and legal context; flows + state matrix; design system a11y docs; component list; auth design.
- **Outputs**: a11y annotations (per screen: heading structure, landmarks, tab order, focus on open/close/error, live-region announcements, accessible names); WCAG checklist results (SC, pass/fail/N/A, evidence); ACR based on VPAT 2.x template [BOOK:ITI VPAT 2.x]; AT test matrix (e.g., NVDA+Firefox/Chrome, JAWS+Chrome, VoiceOver+Safari iOS/macOS, TalkBack+Chrome) [UNVERIFIED as universal standard combos].
- **ACCEPTANCE**:
  - [ ] PRD states the conformance target (default WCAG 2.2 AA) and supported AT/browser matrix.
  - [ ] Zero known AA failures in designs at PRD exit; each SC relevant to the flow is marked pass/N/A with rationale.
  - [ ] Contrast verified: text 4.5:1 (3:1 large), UI components/graphics 3:1, including focus indicators and error colours; information never conveyed by colour alone (1.4.1).
  - [ ] Keyboard path for every interaction; no keyboard traps; focus visible and not obscured by sticky UI (2.4.7, 2.4.11).
  - [ ] Targets ≥24×24 CSS px or spaced (2.5.8); dragging has single-pointer alternative (2.5.7).
  - [ ] Error states: programmatically associated messages, suggestion provided (3.3.1, 3.3.3); status changes announced (4.1.3).
  - [ ] Auth flow has no cognitive function test without alternative — paste allowed in password fields, password managers supported, no unassisted CAPTCHA (3.3.8).
  - [ ] Multi-step flows don't re-ask information (3.3.7); help in consistent place (3.2.6); time limits adjustable (2.2.1).
  - [ ] Tasks include automated a11y checks in CI plus manual keyboard + screen reader test on critical flows in DoD.
- **REFUSAL**:
  - *blocked*: no conformance target or jurisdiction stated for a product with legal exposure; designs without focus/heading/announcement annotations for custom widgets.
  - *rejected*: any known AA violation at a stage exit; "we'll do accessibility in a later phase"; overlays/widgets as a compliance strategy; custom components where a native element would do, with no ARIA pattern specified; architecture choices that make AA impossible (e.g., canvas-only UI without accessibility tree, CAPTCHA-only auth, video without captions pipeline).
  - *out_of_scope*: legal opinion on liability; AAA conformance unless explicitly required; non-digital physical accessibility.
- **Anti-patterns**: relying on Lighthouse score; ARIA sprinkling; alt text = filename; accessibility only for "the blind persona"; treating AA as a stretch goal; disabling zoom.
- **Handoffs**: from Product Designer, Content Designer, Architecture (front-end framework, auth, media pipeline) → to every stage's EXIT gate as a blocking reviewer; to Tasks (a11y DoD per UI task).

### R6. Design Systems Lead / DesignOps Manager
- **Titles**: Design System Lead, Design Technologist, UI Platform Engineer, DesignOps Manager/Program Manager. **Stages**: shared (templates & standards), **architecture** (UI platform decisions), **tasks** (component tasks, handoff format).
- **Mission**: Make quality repeatable: provide the components, tokens, templates, rituals and handoff standards so every team's output is consistent, accessible and buildable.
- **Mindset & principles**: orchestrate people, process and craft to amplify design impact at scale [WEB:https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-ops-definitions/]; shared language first [BOOK:Kholmatova, 2017]; composition over invention [BOOK:Frost, 2016]; tokens as single source of truth, vendor-neutral format [WEB:https://design-tokens.github.io/community-group/format/].
- **OWNS**: component library & documentation (states, a11y, content guidance), token set (DTCG JSON), contribution/governance process, design Definition of Ready / Done, design critique protocol, handoff spec template, versioning/deprecation.
- **DOES NOT OWN**: product-specific flows; product priorities; individual feature code.
- **Inputs**: component requests from product designers; a11y audits; tech stack decisions.
- **Outputs**: component spec (anatomy, props/variants, states, keyboard model, ARIA pattern, content rules, do/don't); `*.tokens.json`; changelog; contribution RFC template; design DoR/DoD checklists.
- **ACCEPTANCE**:
  - [ ] Each component used in the PRD exists in the system with all states documented, or a `net-new` RFC exists.
  - [ ] Every component has documented keyboard interaction and accessible name rules; token colour pairs meet contrast.
  - [ ] Tokens are referenced by semantic name (e.g., `color.text.error`) not raw values in specs.
  - [ ] Handoff package follows the template (flows, state matrix, strings, annotations, tokens, open questions).
- **REFUSAL**:
  - *blocked*: no tech stack/platform decision to bind components to.
  - *rejected*: one-off "snowflake" components duplicating existing ones; specs with hard-coded hex/px; component without documented states or a11y.
  - *out_of_scope*: hiring/career ladders and tooling procurement (real DesignOps duties with no pipeline analogue); brand identity.
- **Anti-patterns**: design system as a sticker sheet with no code parity; governance so heavy teams fork; DesignOps as meeting-scheduling only.
- **Handoffs**: to Architecture (UI platform, token pipeline), Tasks (component tasks), all designers (templates); from a11y specialist (audits).

## Implications for agentic personas

**Split/merge decisions**
- Merge Product Designer + IA into one `ux-designer` worker (IA is a sub-skill at most product companies); keep **content designer** separate — LLMs left alone write generic microcopy, and a dedicated pass on the string catalogue produces measurably more specific errors/empty states.
- Service designer: separate worker invoked by PRD orchestrator only when the product has backstage/human/third-party steps; its blueprint is a **mandatory input to the Architecture entry gate** when present.
- UX researcher (evaluative) becomes a `ux-critic` worker in the PRD EXIT gate: heuristic evaluation + cognitive walkthrough of each critical task, run as ≥2 independent passes (fresh context each) to approximate multiple evaluators; findings severity ≥3 block. It also writes the real usability-test plan as a deliverable for humans.
- Accessibility specialist lives in the **shared cross-cutting pool** and is invoked at PRD exit (design-level SC review), Architecture exit (auth, framework, media, SPA routing/focus), Tasks (a11y DoD per UI task). Single definition, stage-specific checklists.
- DesignOps/design-system lead is mostly **harness, not persona**: its value becomes templates (state matrix, string table, handoff schema), the design DoR/DoD checklists, and a tool that checks components against the inventory. A light `design-system-steward` worker is warranted only if the project has a real component library to check against.

**What LLMs typically get wrong here**
- Happy-path bias: produce flows with no error/empty/loading/partial states, or list "handle errors gracefully" as a requirement. Hard gate: **state-matrix completeness check** (machine-checkable table; empty cells = fail).
- Generic microcopy ("Something went wrong. Please try again.") and inconsistent terminology. Gate: every error string must reference a cause class and a recovery action; glossary diff between strings, PRD and API entity names.
- Claiming WCAG conformance from a text description. An LLM can reason about SCs at design level (contrast values if tokens given, focus order, names, announcements) but cannot verify rendered conformance; must mark SCs `design-reviewed` vs `verified-in-build` and push verification into Tasks DoD (axe in CI + manual SR test).
- Hallucinated research: "users found X confusing" with no study. Gate: evidence tags `real-study | inspection | assumption`; anything not `real-study` cannot be cited as validation.
- Over-designing net-new components and visual detail instead of behaviour. Gate: components must resolve to inventory or a `net-new` RFC.
- Confusing journey map and blueprint; stopping at line of visibility.
- Treating accessibility as a checklist appended at the end, not as constraints on architecture (auth, CAPTCHA, canvas rendering, timeouts, real-time updates and 4.1.3).

**Hard gates (blocking) recommended**
1. PRD EXIT: no flow without ideal/loading/empty/error states (+ partial where lists/paginated); every backend error class mapped. *(rejected if violated)*
2. PRD EXIT: no known WCAG 2.2 AA failure at design level; conformance target and AT matrix stated. *(rejected)*
3. PRD EXIT: no severity 3-4 heuristic finding open. *(rejected)*
4. PRD EXIT: string catalogue complete for all state cells; no placeholder text; glossary consistent. *(rejected)*
5. ARCH ENTRY: blocked if PRD lacks state matrix/error taxonomy (architect cannot design error contract) or lacks a11y target.
6. ARCH EXIT: API error model carries machine codes mappable to user messages; latency budgets compatible with loading-state design; auth satisfies 3.3.8; front-end choice supports accessibility tree & focus management; blueprint backstage steps all have an owning component or documented manual process.
7. TASKS: every UI task's DoD includes its states, strings keys, a11y checks (automated + manual on critical flows); slices are end-to-end per story map.

**Handoff artifact suggestions** (files on disk): `ux/flows.md` (mermaid flowcharts with error edges), `ux/state-matrix.csv`, `ux/strings.csv` (key, state, text, max, placeholders), `ux/glossary.md`, `ux/a11y-annotations.md`, `ux/a11y-checklist.csv` (SC, status, evidence), `ux/heuristic-findings.csv`, `service/blueprint.md`, `ux/usability-test-plan.md`. CSV/structured formats let the EXIT critic and the traceability owner diff mechanically.

**Refusal mapping recap**: blocked = missing persona/platform/a11y target/error taxonomy/backstage owners; rejected = happy-path-only flows, AA violations, generic errors, unsourced "validated" claims, snowflake components, architecture that precludes AA; out_of_scope = GTM/marketing copy, brand identity, legal opinion, translation execution, org design.
